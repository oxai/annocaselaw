You are annotating a judicial opinion in a civil negligence case.

Read the entire supplied opinion. Each sentence is prefixed with its

- Facts
- Procedural History

- Outcome

Select every category that applies to a sentence. Different portions

SCOPE

The Relevant Issues are:
1. The existence or scope of a duty of care.
2. Breach of that duty: whether the defendant was negligent.
3. Contributory negligence: whether the claimant was negligent.

Procedural History and Application of Law to Facts are restricted to
these Relevant Issues. These restrictions do not exclude Facts about
other issues or the court's Outcome.

Discussion solely about causation or damages is outside that scope.
Discussion that merely concerns whether a state entity is immune under
a Tort Claims Act is also outside that scope.

Special liability regimes, including occupiers' liability, employers'
liability, strict liability, and workers' compensation, are within scope
only where the court reasons in terms generalizable to common-law
negligence. State-specific law does not by itself exclude a passage:
include it where it concerns duty or breach.

Property-damage cases are within scope where they turn on the same
negligence elements as physical-injury cases and do not concern pure
economic loss.

A passage addressing a Relevant Issue together with an excluded issue
is not excluded merely because it mentions the excluded issue.

Annotate only the supplied majority opinion. Do not annotate a dissent
or separate concurrence.

FACTS

Events and circumstances that happened before the litigation began,
including:
- The events alleged to give rise to the claim.
- Facts accepted or assumed by the court for its reasoning, including
  conceded facts; they need not have been conclusively established.
- Background about a party's history, behavior, or practices.
- Facts concerning issues outside the Relevant Issues.
- New factual information recounted within the court's reasoning.

Annotate Facts comprehensively, wherever they appear.

Witness testimony relied on by the court is treated as Facts. Where
the court recounts testimony or states that "the evidence establishes"
something, the recounted content is Facts; the introductory phrase is
not separately relevant.

Where witnesses disagree and the court clearly relies on the account
supported by the majority of witnesses, do not annotate the conflicting
minority account as Facts.

Do not annotate evidence that the court rules inadmissible and does not
rely on as Facts. The testimony exception in the Application of Law to
Facts definition below takes precedence where it applies.

PROCEDURAL HISTORY

Statements about:
- The procedural status of the case.
- What happened in the proceedings below.
- The lower court's reasons for its decision, where given.
- The standard of review applied by the present court.

Select these only where they relate to a Relevant Issue.

Descriptions of claims against persons who are not parties to the
present case are not Procedural History for this task.

APPLICATION OF LAW TO FACTS

Application of Law to Facts consists of the minimum necessary statements
of the court's application of law to facts to reach its decision on a
Relevant Issue. It excludes discussion of points not necessary to the
conclusion.

It includes contextual statements that help explain the application of
law to facts, and judicial cues indicating that the court is applying
the law.

It includes discussion of whether evidence has probative value on duty
or breach when that discussion signals the court's reasoning on those
issues.

Where an excerpt of witness testimony is necessary to give proper
meaning to the court's application of law to facts on a point, that
excerpt belongs to Application of Law to Facts rather than Facts.

For contributory negligence, it includes the court's determination of
whether the claimant was negligent and any percentage of contribution.
It excludes discussion solely about whether the claimant's negligence
caused the injury or how damages should be reduced.

Merely reporting a party's argument does not by itself constitute
Application of Law to Facts. Such material qualifies only where it
forms part of the court's own analysis or provides context necessary
to explain that analysis.

OUTCOME

The present court's explicit statement of its outcome, such as finding
for a party, affirming, reversing, remanding, dismissing, modifying, or
otherwise disposing of the case or a claim.

Select the court's statement of the outcome. You may select multiple
statements where the outcome appears more than once.

A description of the lower court's disposition belongs to Procedural
History when that definition applies.
